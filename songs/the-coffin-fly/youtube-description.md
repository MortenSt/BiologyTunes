The Coffin Fly: The Navigator of the Grave – Conicera tibialis | BiologyTunes

How does a fly reach remains buried a metre or more underground? This dark-folk ballad follows the coffin fly, Conicera tibialis, a tiny hump-backed scuttle fly that follows the chemistry of decay down through the soil and raises generation after generation in the dark.

🪰 WHAT THE SONG COVERS
• Scuttle flies: the Phoridae are small, hump-backed flies known for running in quick, jerky bursts. They can fly, but coffin flies often walk and crawl through soil instead.
• The scent of sulfur: decay releases sulfur compounds such as dimethyl trisulfide (DMTS), a well-known attractant for carrion insects. Exactly which cues coffin flies follow underground is still being studied.
• Navigating the soil: coffin flies can reach buried remains through cracks and pores in the soil, and can breed for several generations underground without returning to the surface.
• Forensic evidence: in forensic cases, coffin flies show that a body was buried, or that insects reached it after burial, which helps reconstruct what happened.
• An ancient skill: long before human burials, phorids like these likely exploited dead animals in burrows and other hidden, low-oxygen places. Graves are just a newer version of an old niche.
• A cycle in the dark: eggs, larvae and pupae can all develop in the oxygen-poor space around buried remains.
