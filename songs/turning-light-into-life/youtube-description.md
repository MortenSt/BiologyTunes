Turning Light Into Life – Photosynthesis from Photon to Glucose | BiologyTunes

How does sunlight become the food you eat and the oxygen you breathe? This song follows photosynthesis step by step, from a photon hitting a chloroplast to a finished glucose molecule, and back to the cyanobacteria that started it all billions of years ago.

🌱 WHAT THE SONG COVERS
• The light reactions: in the thylakoid membrane, Photosystem II splits water into O₂, H⁺ and electrons. The electrons flow via cytochrome b₆f to Photosystem I (making NADPH), and the proton gradient drives ATP synthase.
• The equation of life: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂. The oxygen you breathe comes from the split water, not from the CO₂.
• The Calvin cycle: RuBisCO fixes CO₂ in the stroma, and ATP and NADPH turn it into sugar. Six turns of the wheel make one glucose.
• The "dark" reactions: they use no light directly, but their enzymes are switched on by light, so they run mainly in daytime.
• The first oxygen-makers: cyanobacteria-like fossils go back ~3.5 billion years, and molecular clocks put water-splitting at ~3.6–3.2 billion years. Their oxygen was soaked up by iron and volcanic gases for a very long time before it filled the air in the Great Oxidation Event, ~2.4 billion years ago.
• A planetary engine: land plants take up ~120 billion tonnes of carbon a year, and every food chain starts here, even though at most ~4.6–6% of sunlight ends up in biomass.
